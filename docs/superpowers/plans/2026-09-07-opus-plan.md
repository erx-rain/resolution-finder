# Opus plan: how to resolve more without resolving more wrongly

Written 2026-09-07 on Opus, from measured data in the 97-market run
(`correct=18 wrong=1 unresolved=74`). Every claim below is measured, not
assumed. Implementation is Sonnet's; this document is the design.

## The measurement that should drive everything

Root cause of all 74 unresolved (parsed from the run log):

| Cause | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| NO_MATCH — evidence retrieved, cleared relevance, no rule matched | **45** | 61% |
| Corroboration blocked (1 domain only) | 13 | 18% |
| Conflict abstention | 7 | 9% |
| Nothing retrieved | 7 | 9% |
| Deferred structural (crypto price) | 2 | 3% |

And by market shape / truth polarity:

| Shape | Correct | Unresolved | Wrong | Hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| binary, truth = **No** | **0** | **19** | 0 | **0%** |
| binary, truth = Yes | 6 | 25 | 0 | 19% |
| head-to-head (2 options) | 9 | 8 | 1 | **53%** |
| multi (3-8 options) | 2 | 14 | 0 | 12% |
| multi (9+ options) | 1 | 8 | 0 | 11% |

Two facts stand out and they are the same fact:
1. **We have never resolved a "No" — 0 for 19.**
2. **Head-to-head outperforms every other shape by 3-4x.**

## Root cause of both: `_decide_binary` has no elimination path

`_decide_binary` can only ever produce YES — via `BINARY_YES_KEYWORDS`,
`CONSEQUENCE_YES_KEYWORDS`, or `_semantic_yes_signal`. There is no
mechanism anywhere in it to consume positive evidence that the asked-about
outcome did NOT occur.

`_decide_multi_outcome` *does* have one: `_verify_elimination_candidate`,
already built, already NLI-calibrated, already in production. Head-to-head
markets route through that path — which is exactly why they score 53%
while binary-No scores 0%. When Sinner beats Medvedev, multi-outcome
confirms Sinner YES *and* eliminates Medvedev NO from the same sentence.
A binary market has no way to express that second half.

Confirmed by reading the actual retrieved evidence for binary-No markets:
- `will-novak-djokovic-win-the-australian-open-2024` (truth No): retrieved
  evidence includes *"Jannik Sinner ends Novak Djokovic's hunt for 11th
  title - olympics.com"* — explicit and unambiguous.
- `will-a-canadian-team-win-nhl-stanley-cup-782` (truth No): retrieved
  evidence includes *"Frozen in time: No Canadian hockey team has won the
  Stanley Cup Final since 1993 - NBC News"* — the answer in plain English.

Both were returned as UNRESOLVED. Nothing in `_decide_binary` can read them.

## Priority 1 — give `_decide_binary` a NO path (biggest recoverable pool)

**What:** add negative confirmation to `_decide_binary`, mirroring the
existing collect-then-corroborate structure already used for YES.

**Why it's safe** (this is the important part — it must not reopen the
absence-of-evidence coin flip that was deliberately removed in `c4a2b38`):
- This is *positive evidence of a contrary fact*, never inference from
  silence. "Sinner won" is evidence. "We found nothing" remains, correctly,
  not evidence.
- It reuses `_verify_elimination_candidate`, which is already NLI-based,
  already calibrated, and already trusted in production for multi-outcome.
- It must be gated by the **same** `CORROBORATION_MIN_DOMAINS` bar as YES,
  and by the same hedge / wrong-subject / conflict guards.
- If both a YES and a NO confirmation reach the bar, that is a genuine
  conflict → abstain, exactly like `_decide_numeric_threshold` already does
  for its met/not-met split.

**Design sketch** (Sonnet to work out details against real evidence):
- Collect `no_confirmations` alongside the existing `confirmations`, using
  a hypothesis constructed from the market's own subject, e.g. positive
  `"{title} — this did not happen / was ruled out."` Calibrate the exact
  hypothesis wording empirically the way `_winner_hypotheses` was.
- Two distinct sub-cases worth separating, because their risk differs:
  - **(a) Single-winner displacement** — market asks "will X win E", and
    evidence confirms a *different* entity won E. Mechanically checkable
    and low-risk. Covers Djokovic/AO, Canadian-team/Cup, MLB 1-seed,
    Aston-Martin/Mercedes.
  - **(b) Explicit negative assertion** — evidence directly states the
    thing did not happen ("No Canadian team has won since 1993",
    "the debate was cancelled"). Also positive evidence, slightly higher
    reading risk; same corroboration bar applies.
- Do NOT attempt "the event simply never occurred" (shutdown didn't happen,
  impeachment didn't happen). That IS absence-of-evidence and must stay
  unresolved. Roughly 6 of the 19 fall here and should remain unresolved —
  that is correct behaviour, not a gap.

**Expected effect:** the 19 binary-No markets are currently a guaranteed
zero. Even partial recovery is the single largest available gain, and the
mechanism cannot produce a wrong answer that the existing multi-outcome
elimination path wouldn't already produce.

## Priority 2 — cached / replayable eval

**Problem:** retrieval is live and non-deterministic. The Germany market
produced three *different* bugs across three runs. Every before/after number
in the session briefing is confounded by which articles surfaced that hour,
and a 97-market run costs ~90 minutes to produce a number we cannot fully
trust.

**What:** persist fetched article text keyed by (market_id, url) and add a
replay mode to `run_eval.py` that reuses it instead of re-fetching.

**Why it's high leverage:** eval drops from ~90 minutes to seconds, becomes
reproducible, and every subsequent change becomes honestly measurable —
including whether Priority 1 actually helped. With a two-week deadline this
pays for itself almost immediately.

**Caveat:** keep a `--live` path so the cache can be refreshed deliberately;
a stale cache measuring a fixed snapshot is a feature for A/B work but must
not silently become the only truth.

## Priority 3 — the YES-side NO_MATCH long tail (45 cases)

Only attempt *after* 1 and 2, and measure against the cached set.

Observation worth carrying: the six bugs fixed this session
("last year's title game", "doubled its share", "one shy of the record",
"1,039 players scored 308 goals", "on course for", "passed by Congress")
are one bug wearing six masks — **we cannot reliably distinguish "this
sentence is about the topic" from "this sentence asserts the claim."**
Hand-adding phrasings does not scale, and scales worst on the niche
low-coverage markets that are the eventual target.

If an LLM is introduced, the defensible shape remains a **veto/gate**
("is this sentence actually asserting that X?" → yes/no), never an
adjudicator picking between contradictory claims. A wrong veto costs an
unresolved; a wrong adjudication costs a wrong answer.

**Important correction to an earlier recommendation in this session:** an
LLM veto can only ever *remove* candidates. It does nothing for the 0/19
binary-No pool, which is the largest recoverable category. Priority 1 is
strictly more valuable and does not need a model.

## Priority 4 — cheap wins

- **Bing News RSS**: fails its own health check on 17-24% of markets across
  three runs. `search_bing_news_rss` calls `feedparser.parse(url)` with no
  User-Agent, unlike sibling fetches in the same file that spoof one.
  ~30 minutes to test the theory; if Bing contributes nothing, deleting it
  speeds every future run.
- 7 markets retrieved **nothing at all** (`refs=0`), several SCOTUS cases
  among them. That is a query-building problem, not a ranking one — worth a
  look at `build_queries` for case-name-style markets.

## Explicitly DEPRIORITIZED — the 256-token truncation finding

The previous briefing called this the headline finding. **The data says
otherwise and it should be demoted.** Measured refs→ranked counts in the
97-market run: 15→14, 11→11, 10→9, 7→7, 9→8, 6→6. The similarity threshold
rejects almost nothing. Truncation is not costing us candidates.

Further, the fix proposed there (chunk the full description and take max
similarity) may make ranking *worse*: market descriptions are long legal
boilerplate about resolution mechanics, and feeding more of that into the
query embedding drags it away from "news about an event" and toward
"procedural document". If anything is worth testing here it is the opposite
experiment — query on `title` or `title + first sentence` only — and even
that is low priority given the threshold is not the bottleneck.

Not "wrong to have flagged" — it was a real property of the system, and
worth knowing. It is just not where the losses are.

## Sequencing

1. Priority 2 (cached eval) can be done first if Sonnet prefers a
   measurable baseline before changing behaviour — it is the enabler.
2. Priority 1 is the substantive win; measure it on the cached set.
3. Re-measure the shape/polarity table above after Priority 1. If
   binary-No moves off zero without `wrong` rising, that is the signal the
   approach is right.
4. Only then consider Priority 3.

# Addendum A — four more measured findings

## A1. Option names must match in full — this alone explains the wide-field hit rate

`_option_mentioned` requires the *complete* option string to appear
contiguously in the sentence. The only escape hatch is
`TEAM_NICKNAME_ALIASES`, a hand-written table with **four** entries, all
EPL football clubs.

Consequence, measured: `world-series-champion-2025` has the option
"Los Angeles Dodgers". The retrieved, ranked evidence says
*"2025 World Series: **Dodgers**-Blue Jays was a series for the ages"*.
No match. The right answer was retrieved and structurally invisible.

This applies to every wide-field sports market and to tennis markets
("Aryna Sabalenka" vs. evidence saying "Sabalenka"), and largely accounts
for multi(3-8) at 12% and multi(9+) at 11%.

**Fix — self-checking short names, no hand-curation:** derive a short name
per option (normally the final token: Dodgers, Hurricanes, Sabalenka) and
permit it **only if it is unique among that market's own option list**. On
collision, require the full string. Safe by construction, generalises
across every league and to surnames, and retires the 4-entry alias table.
Value is comparable to the binary-NO path — both unlock categories rather
than individual markets.

## A2. close_date = None silently disables the archive pass

`evidence_retriever` gates date-scoped archive search on
`if market.close_date and date.today() > market.close_date`. Markets with
no close date never get it — and for an event years in the past,
current-news search returns nothing useful.

Measured, same tournament, same era:
- close_date present: refs = 15, 26
- close_date absent: refs = **1, 1, 2** (all NO_EVIDENCE / near-empty)

**Fix:** fall back to a year parsed from title/description —
`_market_expected_year` already implements exactly this — or skip the pass
loudly rather than silently. See also the run_eval.py crash on the same
field (ff6369d): this one optional field has now caused two distinct
defects.

## A3. Binary markets are systematically under-retrieved

Query count scales with option count: a 7-option market builds 9 queries,
a 2-option market 4, a **binary market 2**. At MAX_RESULTS_PER_QUERY = 5
that caps binary markets at ~10 candidates versus 45+ for a wide field.

Binary is exactly where we are weakest (0/19 on No, 19% on Yes), and thin
retrieval feeds directly into corroboration blocks — two independent
domains are hard to reach from ten candidates.

**Fix:** generate additional query variants for binary markets (subject
crossed with outcome phrasings, negated forms, key entities from the
description). They currently receive the least search effort despite
needing the most.

## A4. Exclude exact-outcome markets from the headline metric

Several newly-added markets have exact-outcome truths: "Dodgers win 4-1",
"Hurricanes 4-2", "Celtics win 4-1", "#3 Seed", "NL West". An exact series
score is a far harder target than "who won", and these are near-guaranteed
unresolved. Tag them separately so they do not mask real movement in the
numbers we are trying to improve. Measurement hygiene, not a capability
claim.

# Addendum B — strategy for niche markets

Examples given: yellow/red cards, "will a Canadian team win", "will any
country other than X win this tournament". Target profile: markets **not
listed on Polymarket or Kalshi**, so there is no peer market to
cross-check against (peer_market.py is disabled and would be useless here
anyway). The news/data pipeline is the only path, which raises the stakes
on everything below.

## B1. The core realisation: most niche markets are not directly reported

No article states "Player X has accumulated 3 yellow cards this season."
Match reports state "X was booked in the 34th minute." The answer requires
**aggregation across events**, not location of a single asserting
sentence.

Our entire architecture is "find a sentence that asserts the claim." That
structurally cannot answer an aggregation question, no matter how good the
reading gets. This is the most important thing to internalise before
building for niche.

## B2. Decompose: (base fact we already resolve well) + (predicate)

Most niche markets are a *derivation over* a base fact, not a new kind of
fact. Three sub-types, ascending cost:

**Type 1 — set membership.** "Will a Canadian team win the Stanley Cup",
"will a European driver win F1", "will a first-time winner emerge".
Resolve the base fact (who won — head-to-head/multi-outcome, our strongest
path at 53%), then evaluate membership of that winner. The membership
table is small, mostly static, and cheap; a single LLM call ("are the
Florida Panthers a Canadian team?") is also trivially reliable here — this
is lookup, not judgement, so it does not carry the abstention-calibration
risk that made me argue against an LLM adjudicator earlier in this
session.

**Type 2 — complement / negation.** "Will any country other than France
win." Identical to Type 1 with the predicate negated. Note this is the
binary-NO problem wearing a different hat — main-plan Priority 1 serves it
directly.

**Type 3 — aggregation over events.** Yellow/red cards, total goals,
appearances, minutes. Requires a **structured stat source** (league
official stats, FBref/Transfermarkt-class data); news prose cannot answer
it reliably at any reading quality.

**Unification worth noting:** Type 3 is the *same gap* as the deferred
crypto price-history item and the box-office item. Those are not three
separate structural gaps — they are one gap ("resolution source is
structured data, not prose") in three domains. Scoping them as one piece
of work with three adapters is far better value than three bespoke
integrations.

## B3. Corroboration must become tier-aware before niche will work

This resolves the tension flagged at the start of the Opus session:
corroboration demands 2+ independent domains, but niche markets are
exactly where only one source exists. Requiring two independent news
outlets to confirm what a league's own official statistics page states is
not caution — it is a category error.

**Proposal:** weight the bar by source tier instead of counting domains
flatly. One primary/official source (league site, official stats, a
regulator, an exchange's own data) satisfies the bar alone; two
independent credible_backup sources satisfy it as now; general tier
continues to require two.

This keeps the property that actually prevented the wrongs this session —
Cleveland Guardians and Arsenal were both *misread general-tier pages*,
and neither would pass a primary-source test — while making niche markets
resolvable at all.

Do this **after** main-plan Priority 1 and 2, and measure it on the cached
set: it is the one change here that could plausibly raise `wrong`, so it
must not ship unmeasured.

## B4. Retrieval, not reading, is the harder half for niche

"Player X yellow cards" attracts no dedicated news coverage; Google News
RSS is the wrong instrument. Niche retrieval likely needs source-directed
lookup (league site, stats provider, competition results page) chosen from
the market's own named resolution source — which descriptions usually
state explicitly and which we currently do not exploit. The long-deferred
"named-source honouring" backlog item becomes first-class for niche rather
than a nice-to-have.

## B5. Suggested order when niche work begins

1. Type 1 + Type 2 (membership/complement) — reuse the strongest existing
   path, need only a small predicate step.
2. Tier-aware corroboration (B3), measured.
3. Named-source-directed retrieval (B4).
4. Structured-data adapters (B2 Type 3), scoped as one piece of work
   covering stats + crypto + box office rather than three.
